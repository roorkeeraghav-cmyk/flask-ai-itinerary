        <h2>Tourist Attractions</h2>
        <p>Explore Mumbai's iconic landmarks, rich history, and vibrant culture:</p>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>1. Gateway of India</h3>
            <p>An iconic arch monument built during the British Raj, overlooking the Arabian Sea. It's Mumbai's most famous landmark and a starting point for ferry rides.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>2. Elephanta Caves</h3>
            <p>A UNESCO World Heritage Site, these ancient rock-cut caves on Elephanta Island house sculptures dedicated to Shiva. Accessible by ferry from Gateway of India, offering a blend of history and adventure.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>3. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT)</h3>
            <p>A magnificent historic railway station, also a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its Victorian Gothic architecture is breathtaking and a testament to Mumbai's colonial past.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>4. Marine Drive (Queen's Necklace)</h3>
            <p>A 3.6-kilometer-long boulevard along the coast, perfect for a leisurely evening stroll, offering stunning sunset views over the Arabian Sea. Looks like a string of pearls at night.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>5. Colaba Causeway</h3>
            <p>A bustling street market known for its street shopping, handicrafts, souvenirs, and a wide array of cafes and restaurants. Great for experiencing local Mumbai life and food.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>6. Siddhivinayak Temple</h3>
            <p>A revered Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Ganesha, attracting devotees from all walks of life. An important spiritual and cultural site in Mumbai.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="attraction-item">
            <h3>7. Dhobi Ghat</h3>
            <p>The world's largest outdoor laundry, a fascinating and unique cultural experience showcasing hundreds of washermen working tirelessly. A great photo opportunity.</p>
        </div>

            <li class="hotel-item"><strong>The Shalimar Hotel, Kemps Corner:</strong> A more boutique feel, located in a prime South Mumbai area, offering a blend of tradition and modernity.</li>
        </ul>

        <h2>Food Suggestions</h2>
        <p>Mumbai's culinary scene is a delight. Don't miss these local favorites:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Vada Pav:</strong> Mumbai's iconic street food - a spicy potato fritter in a bun.</li>
            <li><strong>Pav Bhaji:</strong> A rich, spicy vegetable mash served with buttered pav (bread rolls).</li>
            <li><strong>Misal Pav:</strong> A spicy curry made of sprouts, topped with farsan, onion, lemon, and served with pav.</li>
            <li><strong>Bombay Sandwich:</strong> A simple yet delicious grilled sandwich with potatoes, tomatoes, onions, and chutney.</li>
            <li><strong>Pani Puri / Bhel Puri / Sev Puri:</strong> Classic Mumbai chaat items, bursting with flavors.</li>
            <li><strong>Kanda Bhaji:</strong> Crispy onion fritters, perfect with a cup of chai.</li>
            <li><strong>Bombil Fry (Bombay Duck):</strong> A local delicacy, especially for seafood lovers.</li>
            <li><strong>Frankie:</strong> A popular Indian wrap, similar to a Kathi roll.</li>
            <li><strong>Modak:</strong> A sweet dumpling, especially revered during Ganesh Chaturthi, but available in sweets shops.</li>
            <li><strong>Mumbai Dabeli:</strong> A sweet and spicy snack made by mixing boiled potatoes with a special dabeli masala.</li>
        </ul>

        <h2>Packing List</h2>
        <p>Pack smart for Mumbai's tropical climate and urban exploration:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Lightweight Clothing:</strong> Cotton or linen clothes are ideal due to high humidity.</li>
            <li><strong>Comfortable Walking Shoes/Sandals:</strong> You'll be doing a lot of walking.</li>
            <li><strong>Umbrella or Light Raincoat:</strong> Essential if traveling during monsoon (June-September).</li>
            <li><strong>Sunscreen, Sunglasses, Hat:</strong> Protect yourself from the strong sun.</li>
            <li><strong>Personal Toiletries & Medications:</strong> Don't forget any essentials.</li>
            <li><strong>Power Bank & Chargers:</strong> Keep your devices charged for navigation and photos.</li>
            <li><strong>Camera:</strong> To capture the vibrant city life.</li>
            <li><strong>Small Backpack/Day Bag:</strong> For daily excursions.</li>
            <li><strong>Photo ID & Important Documents:</strong> (Tickets, hotel bookings, etc. - keep digital copies too).</li>
            <li><strong>Hand Sanitizer & Wet Wipes:</strong> Useful for street food and public places.</li>
        </ul>

        <h2>Travel Tips</h2>
        <ol>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Beat the Traffic:</strong> Mumbai traffic can be intense. Plan your travel time accordingly, especially during peak hours. Use ride-sharing apps like Ola/Uber for convenience.</li>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Stay Hydrated:</strong> Mumbai's humid climate can be dehydrating. Drink plenty of water and fresh juices.</li>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Bargain Smartly:</strong> When shopping at street markets like Colaba Causeway, don't hesitate to bargain for better prices.</li>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Savor Street Food (Wisely):</strong> Mumbai's street food is a must-try. Opt for popular and hygienic vendors, and trust your gut feeling.</li>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Book in Advance:</strong> Especially for flights and popular hotels, booking well in advance can secure better rates and availability.</li>
            <li class="tip"><strong>Carry Small Denominations:</strong> While UPI and cards are widely accepted, having smaller cash notes is handy for street vendors and local transport.</li>
        </ol>

        <h2>Tourist Attractions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>1. Amber Fort (Amer Fort)</h3>
                <p>A majestic fort-palace complex known for its artistic Hindu style elements. Perched high on a hill, it offers stunning views and houses beautiful palaces, halls, and gardens. Enjoy an elephant ride or jeep safari up to the fort.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>2. Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds)</h3>
                <p>An iconic five-story palace built of red and pink sandstone, famous for its unique honeycomb-like exterior with 953 small windows. It allowed royal ladies to observe street festivities without being seen.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>3. City Palace</h3>
                <p>A sprawling complex that houses several palaces, courtyards, gardens, and museums. It reflects a blend of Rajasthani and Mughal architecture, showcasing royal history, artifacts, and exquisite textiles.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>4. Jantar Mantar</h3>
                <p>An astronomical observatory site built by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II. It features a collection of nineteen architectural astronomical instruments, including the world's largest stone sundial, allowing observation of celestial bodies with the naked eye.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>5. Nahargarh Fort</h3>
                <p>Standing on the edge of the Aravalli Hills, Nahargarh Fort provides breathtaking panoramic views of Jaipur, especially at sunset. It's an ideal spot for photography and has a restaurant for a royal dining experience.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>6. Jaigarh Fort</h3>
                <p>Known for housing the 'Jaivana' – the world's largest cannon on wheels. This formidable fort was built for military purposes and offers commanding views of Amber Fort and the surrounding landscape.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>7. Albert Hall Museum</h3>
                <p>Rajasthan's oldest museum, housed in an exquisite Indo-Saracenic building. It displays a rich collection of artifacts including paintings, carpets, ivory, precious stones, sculptures, and an Egyptian mummy.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>8. Patrika Gate</h3>
                <p>A vibrant, relatively new attraction, serving as an entrance to Jawahar Circle Garden. Its arches are adorned with intricate, colorful frescoes depicting various aspects of Rajasthan's culture and history, making it a perfect spot for photography.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="attraction-item">
                <h3>9. Jal Mahal (Water Palace)</h3>
                <p>A beautiful palace seemingly floating in the middle of Man Sagar Lake. While entry to the palace is restricted, its serene beauty and picturesque setting make it a popular spot for photography and leisurely viewing.</p>
            </div>
        </div>

        <h2>Food Suggestions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p>Jaipur is a paradise for food lovers. Don't miss these local delights:</p>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>1. Dal Bati Churma</h3>
                <p>The iconic Rajasthani dish! Hard wheat balls (bati) baked in a clay oven, served with a rich lentil curry (dal) and a sweet crumbly mix of wheat, ghee, and jaggery (churma).</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>2. Laal Maas</h3>
                <p>A fiery red meat curry (traditionally lamb or mutton) prepared with a generous amount of red chilies and a blend of aromatic spices. A must-try for non-vegetarian enthusiasts.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>3. Ghewar</h3>
                <p>A traditional Rajasthani sweet, especially popular during festivals. It's a disc-shaped dessert made from flour, soaked in sugar syrup, and often topped with mawa, nuts, or rabri.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>4. Pyaz Kachori</h3>
                <p>A popular savory snack. Deep-fried, flaky pastry filled with a spicy onion mixture, best enjoyed with mint and tamarind chutneys.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>5. Mirchi Bada</h3>
                <p>Another beloved snack, featuring large green chilies stuffed with a spicy potato filling, dipped in gram flour batter, and deep-fried until golden.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>6. Rajasthani Thali</h3>
                <p>For a complete culinary experience, opt for a traditional Rajasthani Thali, offering a platter of various curries, breads, rice, and sweets.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="food-item">
                <h3>7. Kulfi</h3>
                <p>A traditional Indian frozen dairy dessert, similar to ice cream but denser and creamier, available in various flavors like pistachio, mango, rose, and malai.</p>
            </div>
        </div>

            <p>Jaipur's cuisine is a delightful blend of rich flavors and traditional recipes. Don't miss these local favorites:</p>

                <li><strong>Pyaaz Kachori:</strong> A crispy, deep-fried pastry filled with a spicy onion mixture, perfect for breakfast or a snack.</li>

                <li><strong>Laal Maas:</strong> A fiery red meat curry prepared with a unique blend of spices, a treat for non-vegetarian food lovers.</li>
                <li><strong>Gatta Curry:</strong> A vegetarian delicacy made with gram flour dumplings cooked in a spicy yogurt-based gravy.</li>
                <li><strong>Ker Sangri:</strong> A traditional Rajasthani dry vegetable dish made from local berries and beans, rich in flavor.</li>
                <li><strong>Mawa Kachori:</strong> A sweet variant of kachori, filled with mawa (condensed milk) and dry fruits, usually served hot.</li>
                <li><strong>Lassi:</strong> A refreshing yogurt-based drink, sweet or salted, often topped with cream and dry fruits.</li>

    <section>
        <h2>Food Suggestions (Must-Try Local Delicacies)</h2>
        <p>Jaipur is a paradise for food lovers. Don't miss these authentic Rajasthani flavors:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Dal Baati Churma:</strong> The quintessential Rajasthani dish, a wholesome meal of baked wheat balls (baati), lentil curry (dal), and sweet crumbled wheat (churma).</li>
            <li><strong>Laal Maas:</strong> A fiery red meat curry prepared with mutton, yogurt, and a blend of potent spices. A treat for non-vegetarian spice lovers.</li>
            <li><strong>Gatte ki Sabzi:</strong> A popular vegetarian curry made with gram flour dumplings (gatte) cooked in a spicy yogurt-based gravy.</li>
            <li><strong>Pyaaz Kachori:</strong> A crispy, deep-fried pastry filled with a spicy onion mixture, best enjoyed with a side of sweet and spicy chutneys.</li>
            <li><strong>Mawa Kachori:</strong> A sweet variant of kachori, filled with khoya (reduced milk solids) and dry fruits, often dipped in sugar syrup.</li>
            <li><strong>Ghevar:</strong> A disc-shaped sweet cake made from flour and soaked in sugar syrup, typically enjoyed during festivals like Teej and Raksha Bandhan.</li>
            <li><strong>Lassi:</strong> A refreshing yogurt-based drink, often thick and creamy, available in sweet, salty, or fruit flavors.</li>
            <li><strong>Mirchi Bada:</strong> A popular spicy snack made from a large green chili stuffed with a spicy potato filling, coated in gram flour batter and deep-fried.</li>
        </ul>
    </section>

        <h2>Travel Tips</h2>
